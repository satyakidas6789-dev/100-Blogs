# How to Get Your First Internship as a BCA or B.Tech Student in 2026

"I don't have experience" — this is the paradox every fresher faces. You need experience to get an internship, but you need an internship to get experience. Here's how to break the cycle.

## Why Internships Are Critical

An internship:
- Gives you real-world experience that classes can't provide
- Builds your resume and LinkedIn
- Often converts into a full-time offer (30–50% of interns get PPO)
- Pays you (even stipends of ₹5,000–25,000/month help)

## The Truth About "No Experience"

Recruiters at internship stage aren't expecting experience. They're looking for:
1. **Learning ability** (can you figure things out?)
2. **Eagerness** (do you genuinely want to work here?)
3. **Basic skills** (Python/Java, one project, Git basics)

Build these three things and you have what you need.

## Where to Apply for Internships

### Dedicated Platforms
- **Internshala**: biggest India internship platform (paid and unpaid)
- **Unstop**: campus and open internships
- **LinkedIn Jobs**: filter for "Internship"
- **Wellfound (formerly AngelList)**: startup internships

### Direct Company Applications
- FAANG India: Google STEP, Microsoft Explore
- Mid-size companies: check careers pages directly
- Startups: cold email + LinkedIn outreach works well

### College Resources
- Placement cell: often has exclusive internship listings
- Alumni network: reach out to seniors (they refer)
- Professor connections: professors often know companies

## Building the Minimum Viable Application

You need:

**1. One Real Project**
Not a tutorial copy — a project you can explain:
- Why you built it
- What problem it solves
- What tech decisions you made

**2. A GitHub Profile**
Clean profile, projects with READMEs, regular commit history.

**3. A Focused Resume (1 page)**
- Name, contact, LinkedIn, GitHub
- Education (college, CGPA)
- Skills section (Python, Java, SQL, relevant frameworks)
- 1 project with 2–3 bullet points
- Certifications (Coursera, Classroom Tech, Google)

**4. A Brief Cover Email**
3 sentences: who you are, what you built, why you want THIS company.

## Internship Application Strategy

| Channel | Effort | Success Rate |
|---------|--------|-------------|
| Cold email to founders | High | Medium-High |
| LinkedIn outreach to managers | Medium | Medium |
| Internshala applications | Low | Low (high volume) |
| College alumni referral | Medium | High |

Best approach: combine all four.

## The Cold Email Formula

*Subject: Internship Application — Python Developer (BCA, Final Year)*

*"Hi [Name], I'm [Your name], a final-year BCA student at [College] with a strong interest in [Company's domain]. I've built [brief project description] using [tech]. I'd love to contribute to [specific thing the company does]. Could we set up a 15-minute call? My GitHub: [link]"*

Keep it under 100 words.

## After Getting the Internship

- Ask for real work, not just coffee-fetching
- Maintain a daily log of what you learn
- Network with full-time employees
- Aim for a PPO (Pre-Placement Offer)
