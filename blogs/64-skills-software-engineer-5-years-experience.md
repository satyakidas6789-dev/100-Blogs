# Skills Every Software Engineer Should Have After 5 Years of Experience

Five years into your career, you're expected to operate differently than a fresher. Companies expect you to think in systems, not just functions. Here's the complete picture.

---

## Technical Skills (Hard Requirements)

### Architecture and System Design
At 5 years, you should be able to design systems, not just implement them:
- Design a system to handle 1 million users
- Choose between SQL and NoSQL with clear reasoning
- Design for scalability, fault tolerance, and security
- Understand microservices vs monolith trade-offs

### Deep Expertise in One Stack
You should own your stack completely:
- **Java Full Stack:** Spring Boot internals, JVM tuning, Spring Security, microservices
- **Python/ML:** Production ML pipelines, MLOps, model optimization
- **Full Stack Web:** Advanced React patterns, Node.js clustering, API design

### DevOps and Infrastructure
At 5 years, "it's DevOps's job" is no longer acceptable:
- Own your CI/CD pipeline
- Understand Docker and Kubernetes
- Monitor your services (set up alerts, not just read them)

### AI Integration
In 2026, any senior developer without AI integration experience is behind:
- Know how to call LLM APIs and handle responses
- Understand when AI is appropriate vs over-engineered
- Have built at least one AI-powered feature in production

---

## Technical Leadership Skills

- **Code review:** Mentor juniors through code reviews (teach, don't just correct)
- **Architecture discussions:** Contribute meaningfully to design decisions
- **Technical documentation:** Write design docs that last beyond your tenure
- **Estimation:** Accurately estimate complexity (not just time)

---

## Non-Technical Skills (The Career Multiplier)

These are what separate ₹15 LPA developers from ₹40 LPA ones:

### Communication
- Explain technical concepts to non-technical stakeholders
- Write clear project status updates
- Articulate trade-offs in business terms

### Ownership
- Drive projects to completion without being managed
- Proactively identify and fix problems before they escalate

### Mentoring
- Raise the skill level of your team
- Be the person junior developers go to

### Strategic Thinking
- Understand the business context of what you're building
- Push back on bad technical decisions with clear reasoning
